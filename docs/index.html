05 // research
RTC Timing Drift Analysis in Zephyr RTOS on Nordic nRF52840
Graduate Research — Minnesota State University, Mankato · Supervisor: Dr. Nannan He
Completed

Investigated real-time clock timing drift behavior in Zephyr RTOS on the Nordic nRF52840 DK, specifically analyzing the impact of Product Anomaly 179 (spurious RTC compare events) and Zephyr's MIN_CYCLES_FROM_NOW = 3 mitigation strategy.

  • Experimental design: Implemented full Zephyr firmware across three margin configurations — stock (MIN_CYCLES_FROM_NOW = 3), moderate (+2), and conservative (+5) — measuring per-wakeup timing error over 10,000+ sleep-wakeup cycles via UART timestamp capture.
  • Key finding: Observed drift was not caused by Anomaly-179 double-fires as hypothesized, but by deterministic RTC tick quantization from k_uptime_get()'s 1ms resolution — tick boundaries cause systematic rounding error in sleep duration calculations.
  • Developed Python UART capture and statistical analysis scripts — automated data collection, drift histogram generation, and per-configuration comparison plots.
  • Produced a complete graduate research paper documenting methodology, results, and implications for low-power RTOS timer accuracy in battery-constrained embedded systems.
